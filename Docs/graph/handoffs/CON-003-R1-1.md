## 7. Decisions and assumptions

Per the task packet's Section 9 ("Known open questions — do not resolve
silently"), all three are recorded explicitly, not silently decided:

1. **Framework/build-system and partition/bootloader ownership (open
   question 1).** Still unresolved. `manifest.rs::build_manifest` declares
   exactly one `ToolchainDescriptor` whose every field states, in the value
   itself, that it is unresolved (`name:
   "unconfirmed_build_toolchain"`, `pinned_version:
   "provisional-unconfirmed-pending-human-decision"`, `integrity_source`
   pointing at the exact unresolved spec item, `license: "unconfirmed"`).
   This is a deliberate choice over silent omission: an omitted entry could
   be misread by a future reader as "no toolchain needed" rather than "not
   yet decided." No ESP-IDF (or any other framework) name/version is pinned
   anywhere in this crate.
2. **Recovery-access and known-good-artifact policy (open question 2).**
   Still undecided. `rollback` is fully declared and dispatchable
   (`capabilities.rs` declares it; `connector.rs::dispatch_rollback_preflight`
   runs and is exercised by two tests), but its `preflight` always fails
   honestly with `recovery_policy_undecided`, explicitly citing
   `Docs/CONNECTOR_SPEC.md` §10 and this packet's own Section 9 — it never
   touches the transport, so it cannot even look like it attempted an
   unauthorized recovery action. `flash_artifact` and `restart`, by
   contrast, do have a real (best-effort, non-guaranteed) `recover()` that
   performs an actual diagnostic transport call before still honestly
   reporting failure when no verified-safe outcome exists — this asymmetry
   is deliberate and documented in `connector.rs`'s module doc and each
   dispatch function's own comment.
3. **`IdentityConfidence` (open question 3).** This connector's own
   documented choice: `dispatch_identify_verify` reports
   `IdentityConfidence::Moderate`, not `Strong`, for the chip-ID evidence it
   attaches. Reasoning recorded in `connector.rs`'s module doc: a real
   ESP32 ROM chip-ID query would likely justify `Strong` in production, but
   this node only proves the *structure* against a fake transport (this
   node's hard forbidden scope forbids any real device I/O), so no physical
   evidence yet exists that the real transport implementation will actually
   observe chip-ID material with that strength. This is explicitly not an
   inherited or final policy — a future node with real hardware evidence
   may justifiably raise it.
